The cheapest next job on Upwork is the client who already paid you. They have seen a draft, they have a contract history with you, and they do not need another proposal from a stranger. Most freelancers throw that away. They submit the last files, say "let me know if you need anything," and open the public feed again.

Get the next piece of work on purpose. Close this contract properly, ask for the review at the right time, and offer one specific follow-on. Not a vague "happy to help with future projects."

## Finish the thing they hired

The follow-on starts with a clean end. Submit the milestone with a short list of what is included and a link that works. Tell them how to request the revision round you already priced. If they still owe you text or a login, say that the submission is waiting on that item. Do not mark a site done when the contact form posts nowhere.

Clients remember the last week more than the proposal. A tidy handoff makes the next yes easier. A messy handoff makes the review your next problem.

## Ask for the review after they have the result

When the work is submitted and the client has had a chance to use it, ask for a review in one or two sentences. Thank them for the clear feedback if they gave any. Do not send a script for them to paste. People can smell a coerced five stars, and Upwork's review system is also not something you should try to script around.

If they are happy, they will often leave the review when the contract closes. If they are slow, one reminder is enough. A second and third reminder turns a fine project into an awkward one.

You may leave them a review too. Keep it specific and calm. Public revenge for a picky client is a review future clients can read.

## Offer one next piece, with a price

Look at what you already saw on their site. The follow-on should be obvious from the work, not from your wish list.

- The landing page is live, and they still have no real services page
- The brochure site is up, and the contact form has no confirmation message
- The small app slice works, and they asked twice about a second record type

Pick one. Write three lines: what you would add, how long it takes, what it costs, and that it can be a new milestone or a new offer. Send it in the contract thread after the handoff, not three weeks later when they have forgotten your name.

One offer is a service. A menu of six retainers is a brochure they did not request. If they say no, stop. You can check in much later when you have a real reason ("the page we shipped still links to the old menu"). Do not monthly-newsletter them from an Upwork contract.

## Keep the next job on the platform

Add the milestone or accept the new offer on Upwork. Same rules as the first job: funded milestone or tracked hours, scope written down, files in the contract. The trust you earned is not a reason to move the invoice to a private channel. It is a reason the second contract will be easier to scope.

If they ask to continue off Upwork, decline and point at the new milestone. You already know how to say it. The fee on a second $1,000 job is still cheaper than a dispute with no contract.

## Use the review as proof, not as a personality

A specific review ("delivered the five-page site on time and fixed the mobile nav") helps the next stranger. A generic "great guy" review helps less. You cannot dictate their words. You can make the delivery easy to describe by doing one clear thing.

Put that finished URL in your portfolio if you are allowed to show it. Crop a thumbnail that looks like the site, not like a certificate. The next client from the feed should see the same kind of page this client just paid for.

## Timing that does not feel like a chase

Send the follow-on in the same breath as the handoff, while they are looking at the finished work. Waiting a month to "not be pushy" mostly means they have moved on. Being pushy is a second and third pitch, or a retainer they did not ask for. One priced next step, once, is ordinary business.

If they say they need a week to look, leave it. Put a reminder on your own calendar if you want, and do not ping them before that week ends. The review can still be requested when the contract closes, even if they never buy the extra page.

## When there is no next job

Some clients truly need one site and nothing else. Thank them, close the contract, and leave the door as that single offer you already sent. Do not invent a maintenance retainer they do not need. A retainer with no defined monthly task becomes resentment on both sides.

Then go back to the job feed with a portfolio that is one site stronger. The client who hired you once has done their part. The next stranger is a new proposal, and it should point at the work you just finished, not at a promise that you are "available for anything."
