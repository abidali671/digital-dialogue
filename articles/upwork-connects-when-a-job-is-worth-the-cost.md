CONTENT:

Connects are what you pay to send an Upwork proposal. The price of one Connect and the number a job demands both move, so the only number that matters is the one on that job today. A post that costs a handful of Connects and a post that costs a pile are not the same decision, even when the descriptions look alike.

This is a filter for web developers who already know their offer. It will not help if the profile is empty. Connects cannot compensate for that.

## Look at the cost before the story

Open the job and find the Connects required before you fall for the client's mission statement. Write the cost down. If you buy Connects in a pack, you can turn that count into money with the price shown at checkout. You do not need a perfect formula. You need to notice when a bid costs as much as a meal and the budget for the site is a rounding error.

Some jobs ask for more Connects because they are popular or because the client boosted the post. Popular does not mean well scoped. A crowded post for "modern website, best price" is often an expensive way to be one of forty similar replies.

## A job earns the bid only if the work is real

Read the whole post once. Then answer these without improvising.

- Is there a deliverable you can name (landing page, five pages, a bug in the contact form)?
- Does the stack match what you actually ship?
- Is the budget close to your price, or can you offer a smaller slice that fits their number?
- Are the files, the brand, and the text already available, or are you supposed to invent a company from a sentence?
- Is the client hiring on Upwork, or recruiting you toward email, Telegram, or a free sample before any offer?

Four yes answers and a no on the last question is a possible bid. Anything else is a Connects leak. Beginners talk themselves into the leak because the post used words like "long-term" and "great opportunity." Those words are free to type.

## Red flags that make the Connects a donation

Walk away when the post asks you to share work "to see your speed" with no pay. Walk away when the budget is a full brochure site at a price that would not cover a day. Walk away when they want the contract off Upwork "to save fees." You would be paying Connects for a client who already plans to break the rules that protect you.

Also walk away from posts that are five products in one: site, logo, copy, ads, and a mobile app, with a single small budget. You can reply with a narrower offer, but only if you are willing to state that narrower offer and let them ignore you. Do not quietly accept the whole list.

## When a higher Connects cost can still make sense

Pay more Connects when the job is specific, the budget fits a page list you have priced before, and your portfolio shows that kind of site. A clear $2,000 brochure-site post can justify a more expensive bid. A vague post does not become justified because you are afraid the good jobs will disappear.

Boosting is the same test with a higher bill. If you would be fine losing the base Connects and getting no reply, you might boost. If losing them would sting, do not. The boost does not improve a proposal that never mentions the client's pages.

Invites are different. If a client invites you, read whether a proposal still spends Connects. Sometimes the invite is just a notification. Sometimes responding still costs. Check the button before you click it.

## A weekly cap so the balance survives

Decide a Connects budget for the week before you open the feed. Ten well-read jobs, two proposals. Or whatever small number you can review properly. When the cap is hit, stop. More scrolling does not create a better client. It creates a tired yes.

Keep a line in a note for each proposal: Connects spent, budget on the post, and whether anyone replied. After a few weeks the waste is obvious. The jobs that felt urgent and went nowhere should come off your list as a pattern, not as a moral failure.

## When you are unsure, do not bid "just in case"

Uncertainty is not a reason to spend the Connects and decide later. Later, the proposal is already sent. If you cannot tell whether the job is a landing page or a product, ask nothing and close it, or send a proposal that prices only the landing page and says the rest is out of scope. Both are decisions. A vague bid that hopes they will clarify is how you pay twice: once in Connects, again in a messy contract if they actually hire you.

## Spend Connects like a fee, then write the proposal

If the job passes the filter, write the proposal the same day, with their pages and your price. Sitting on a saved job until tomorrow often means the client has already picked someone, and your Connects are gone anyway if you already submitted. If you have not submitted, closing the tab costs nothing.

The fee you pay to bid is small next to a bad contract. The filter is there so you do not collect bad contracts in the name of "staying active." Active and selective is the version that still has Connects left on Friday.
